Science literacy · built by a high schooler

Learn to read the science — and judge whether it's any good.

Every other tool makes research papers easier to believe. FirstPaper makes you better at deciding whether to. Read a real paper, section by section, at your level — then learn to catch what's weak in it.

Try it — one line from a real study

Carriers of the A-allele reported higher morning alertness than non-carriers. These findings suggest the variant drives morning alertness and may represent a target for alertness-boosting supplements.

The verb doesn't match the design. They measured a correlation — two things lining up. But "drives" claims a cause. Nothing in a study like this can support that word. Once you start reading verbs this way, you can't stop. That's the whole skill.
The method

Three moves, on every paragraph.

Most tools translate a paper and hand it back. That teaches you to trust whatever gets simplified. This teaches you to interrogate it.

01 — Read
What it says
The real paper, verbatim, with a short plain-language translation beside any paragraph that loses you. Never a summary of what's wrong — just what it means.
02 — Check
Did it land?
Quick questions so you can't drift forward half-understanding. Instant feedback, no punishment for getting it wrong.
03 — Judge
Is it any good?
The part everyone else skips. Sample size, controls, correlation vs cause, who funded it. You commit to an answer before you see the explanation.
