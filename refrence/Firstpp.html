Before you start

You're about to read a real research paper. All of it.

Not a summary, not a simplified version — the actual published text, exactly as the scientists wrote it, figures included. That will feel hard in places. It's supposed to. Here's how this works and what you'll walk away with.

What makes this different

Every other AI tool takes a paper and makes it easier to believe. It translates the hard parts and hands them back, which quietly teaches you to trust whatever gets explained to you. FirstPaper does the opposite: it teaches you to judge whether a paper deserves believing. That skill transfers to every paper you ever read — and to a lot that aren't papers.

What you'll actually do
  • Read the paper section by section, verbatim.
  • Check your understanding — 5 questions.
  • Judge the science — 5 harder questions on whether it holds up.
  • Recap in plain English, with no jargon at all.
What you'll walk away able to do
  • Tell a claim from a hedge by reading the verb.
  • Say what a control is actually for.
  • Spot when a sample is too small — and what rescues it.
  • Read a figure before you read the conclusion.
  • Check who paid, and whether it shows.
◆ The green dots — read this bit
?

Every paragraph of the paper has a green dot beside it. The dot is help, and help is always available — but it asks you to try first.

Tap a dot and you don't get the answer straight away. You get a prompt: say this paragraph back in your own words, in your head. Then two choices:

I've got it — you understood it yourself. +8 XP. This is the one worth chasing.

Show me anyway — you get a plain-language translation of that exact paragraph. Costs 5 from your insight bonus, never from your XP, and never drops below zero.

Asking for help is never wrong. The explanations exist because papers are genuinely hard, and a paragraph you don't understand teaches you nothing. But skipping a paragraph entirely earns you nothing at all — the dots are how you engage with the reading.
